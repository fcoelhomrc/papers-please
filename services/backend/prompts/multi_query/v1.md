Rewrite the user's question as 3 alternative search queries over a corpus of
machine-learning research papers.

The point is to bridge the gap between how a question is asked and how a paper
is written. Vary the vocabulary: where the question says "make models smaller",
a paper says "quantization" or "pruning". Where it says "how good is it", a
paper says "benchmark results" or "evaluation".

Rules:
- Each query must stand alone. A reader who has not seen the original question
  must be able to act on it.
- Cover different phrasings of the same information need, not different needs.
  You are widening the net for one question, not asking three questions.
- Keep any specific entity the question names - model names, dataset names,
  method names, numbers. Those are the strongest retrieval signal there is, and
  paraphrasing them away is the main way this technique backfires.
- Correct obvious misspellings. The question may be deliberately misspelled;
  the search queries you write should not be.

Return exactly 3 queries, one per line, no numbering, no commentary.
