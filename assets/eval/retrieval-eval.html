Retrieval evaluation

100 curated questions over a 100-paper, 11,450-chunk corpus. Every configuration below costs nothing to run — no LLM is involved, so the whole grid is one pass of the index.

Recall against retrieval depth

Keyword search leads at every depth, and the gap widens with k. Hybrid tracks semantic almost exactly — keyword carries a tenth of dense retrieval's weight in the fusion, so hybrid is dense retrieval with a rounding error.

Ranking quality against retrieval depth

nDCG is the selection metric — it rewards putting the right chunk near the top, not merely returning it somewhere. The mode ordering is the same as for recall, and keyword's lead survives it.

Does reranking earn its cost?

At matched output size — the only fair comparison, since recall rises with k mechanically. Reranking a pool beats plain hybrid at every depth by +0.015 to +0.030 nDCG, and every one of those intervals spans zero. Plain keyword beats the reranked pipeline outright from k=3 up, without a cross-encoder. Dots rather than bars: at a zero baseline these four rows are visually identical, and the difference is the point.

Where retrieval actually fails

Split by question type, the mean hides two populations. Single-hop retrieval is close to solved; multi-hop needs every one of 2–3 gold chunks and sits three times lower. Bars show 95% confidence intervals — these do not overlap.

Reranking: pool size against what it returns

Each point is one of 48 reranker configurations, positioned by what it returns and how well it ranks. A wider candidate pool lifts recall — the cross-encoder can only reorder what the first stage fetched — but nDCG barely moves.
